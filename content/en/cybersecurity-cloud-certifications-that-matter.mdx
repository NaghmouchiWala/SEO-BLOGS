One question comes up constantly among people considering a switch into cybersecurity or cloud: "which certification should I start with?" The honest answer is that there's no universal certification — there are coherent career paths, and there are piles of badges that open no doors because they don't tell a recruiter a clear story.

## Why some certifications carry more weight than others

Not all certifications are evaluated equally by employers. A certification with international recognition, a rigorous exam, and regularly updated content carries real weight on a resume. A certification earned after a two-hour course with no meaningful test carries almost none, no matter how nice it looks on LinkedIn.

That's why providers like [Go2Code](https://go2code.tn) build their catalog around certifications with genuine market recognition: AWS Cloud Practitioner for cloud, CompTIA Security+ for cybersecurity fundamentals, CompTIA PenTest+ for penetration testing, or Microsoft Azure Fundamentals. These aren't arbitrary choices — they're certifications tech recruiters actually recognize and value, in Tunisia and internationally.

## The trap of the isolated certification

A common mistake among beginners: collecting disconnected certifications with no logical path between them. A cybersecurity certification followed by a graphic design certification followed by a project management certification tells a recruiter no coherent story. It reads as someone still searching, not someone building expertise.

A coherent path looks more like this: an intro to cybersecurity, then CompTIA Security+ for the fundamentals, then a specialization — pentesting, cloud security, or governance — based on what clicks along the way. That's the approach enabled by a structured catalog like [Go2Code's](https://go2code.tn/tn/fr/formations?cat=Intelligence%20Artificielle), which offers progressive tracks rather than isolated courses.

## Cloud and cybersecurity increasingly overlap

A notable trend in the tech job market: the line between cybersecurity and cloud computing keeps blurring. Most modern infrastructure runs on AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud, and securing that infrastructure requires understanding both domains at once.

That's why paths combining a cloud certification (AWS, Azure) with a security certification (CompTIA Security+, pentesting) carry particular weight in today's market — they map directly onto the skills companies are actively hiring for to secure their cloud infrastructure, a need that keeps growing.
