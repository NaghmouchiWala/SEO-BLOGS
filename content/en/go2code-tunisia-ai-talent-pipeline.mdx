Every year, computer science graduates across Tunisia run into the same wall: a solid academic degree, but little hands-on experience with the tools companies actually rely on day to day. That gap between classroom theory and market-ready skills remains one of the biggest barriers to tech employability in the country — and in many others facing the same disconnect.

That's precisely the gap organizations like [Go2Code](https://go2code.tn) are built to close, with an approach that favors intensive, hands-on practice over lecture-heavy theory.

## A curriculum built around "doing"

Go2Code's promise is summed up in three words on its site: *"Learn. Build. Launch."* It's not just a tagline — the training catalog is structured around intensive bootcamps rather than theory-only courses stretched over months without real application.

The catalog spans a wide range: artificial intelligence (from an [AI developer bootcamp](https://go2code.tn/tn/fr/formations?cat=Intelligence%20Artificielle) to advanced AI engineering), cloud and DevOps (AWS, Azure, Kubernetes), cybersecurity (CompTIA Security+, penetration testing), software development (React, ISTQB testing, game development), data science (Python, Power BI), digital marketing, and UX/UI design.

That breadth isn't accidental — it mirrors market reality, where a tech company rarely needs just one type of profile. It needs a cross-disciplinary team covering development, data, cloud, and security all at once.

## Betting on internationally recognized certifications

One detail sets Go2Code apart from many local training providers: a strong emphasis on internationally recognized certifications — NVIDIA for AI, AWS for cloud, CompTIA for cybersecurity, and PMP for project management. For a young Tunisian professional targeting both the local market and international opportunities, that kind of credential often carries more weight on a resume than a generalist diploma.

The school even backs this with a [certification guarantee](https://go2code.tn/tn/fr/garantie-certification), a commitment that holds both the learner and the training provider accountable for the actual outcome, not just classroom attendance.

## Preparing the next generation, literally

One of the more distinctive programs in the catalog is [GO2CODE AI Junior](https://go2code.tn/ai-junior), aimed at children aged 8 to 14. In a world where artificial intelligence is already reshaping entire professions, teaching kids to understand and build with AI — rather than just consume it through apps — is a position that clearly anticipates the skills this generation will need a decade from now.

The program is built around a simple, low-friction format ("nothing to fill in") that lowers the barrier for parents, with transparent monthly or quarterly pricing.

## Two hackerspaces, strong local roots

Unlike platforms that go fully online, Go2Code maintains a physical presence through its [hackerspaces in Tunis](https://go2code.tn/tn/fr/a-propos) — Ain Zaghouen Nord and Jardin de Carthage — while also offering fully online tracks. That dual approach captures both learners who thrive on the energy and structure of an in-person space, and those who need the flexibility of remote learning.

## The link to Tunisia's AI ecosystem

Go2Code doesn't train in a vacuum. It's part of the same group as [Galylio](https://galylio.com), the Tunisian company that builds custom AI systems for businesses. That proximity creates a natural bridge between training and real-world application: the skills taught at Go2Code — machine learning, full-stack development, cloud engineering — are exactly what companies like Galylio look for when building their own engineering teams.

It's a model that makes sense for Tunisia's broader tech ecosystem: the more institutions genuinely train people in market-relevant skills, the larger the pool of job-ready talent local companies can draw from, instead of having to build everything in-house from scratch.

## Who Go2Code is actually built for

Three profiles stand out clearly from the catalog:

- **Complete beginners** looking for a fast track into tech through intensive bootcamps
- **Working professionals** who want to upskill in a specific area — generative AI, cloud, data — without starting from zero
- **Parents** who want to give their kids a head start on skills that will define their future careers

In an increasingly competitive Tunisian tech job market, the real question isn't "should I train in AI and tech" anymore — it's "with which provider, and with what level of accountability for the actual outcome." That's where concrete guarantees — recognized certifications, industry-experienced instructors, and a dual in-person/online format — separate training that just sits on a resume from training that genuinely changes a career trajectory.
